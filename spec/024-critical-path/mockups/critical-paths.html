Critical Paths — redesign & placement

Mockups for spec/024-critical-path. All numbers assume the phase-1 model fix: the Monte Carlo traces the chain that actually drove the finish date in each iteration, so every path carries a criticality index (% of simulations it was on the driving chain) and a split of its span into working time versus time queued behind other work in this plan.

Two definitions this report rests on
  • Chain span — the days from the first epic's start to the last epic's end, measured at the selected uncertainty percentile so it agrees with the autoscheduler's bars. It is not the plan length: the chain that drives the finish date in any single iteration runs from day 0 to the last day, so that quantity is the same for every row and useless as a column. Nor is it today's totalDaysInCriticalPath, which sums per-issue P80s and is not a duration at all.
  • Queued — startDay − earliestStartTimeFromBlockers(issue): the gap between an epic being unblocked and its team's track having room (schedule.ts:67 → workplan.ts:117). The thing occupying that track is another epic in this plan. Work the team does outside the plan is invisible to the scheduler — it enters only through pointsPerDayPerTrack and parallelWorkLimit — so every queued figure here is a floor. Real waiting is equal or worse, never better.

Series palette #0c66e4 / #b65c02 validated with the dataviz validator — CVD ΔE 30.2 (protan), normal-vision ΔE 34.2, all six checks pass. Status tokens (--blocked-color, --warning-color, …) are reserved and deliberately not reused here; the two series are composition, not state, so they carry a legend.

Why row 4 matters

In the current report (../critical-path.png) "Tablet Mode UI Enhancements" is row 2, because 132 days is the second-largest number. Under criticality it drops to row 4 at 12% — it is long but it has slack, so it rarely decides the finish date. This is the same failure as the A→[9,9,9]→10 vs 10→10→10 case: ranking by a deterministic day-sum names the wrong long pole.

2 · Team load callout

idea.md asked for "a little call out for the teams that have the most total working days." Raw total working days alone can't say whether a team is over-capacitated — 300 days of work inside a 900-day plan is fine. What the model can say is how much of the plan's length a team's own queue fills, which is also exactly where the orange segments above come from. One series, one color; the largest is emphasized rather than value-ramped.

This is not a capacity verdict, and the wording has to say so. The scheduler only sees the epics loaded into the plan. A team also carrying substantial unmodeled work is already past 100% while this panel still reads 92% — so the panel reports plan load and names the exclusion, rather than declaring anyone over-capacitated. The way to model out-of-plan load is to lower that team's pointsPerDayPerTrack in team configuration.

5 · Decisions taken

  • Fix the model before the presentation. Ranking by deterministic blocksWorkDepth can name the wrong long pole (see "Why row 4 matters"), so phase 1 adds the per-iteration trace and phase 2 builds this UI on top of it.
  • Criticality all the way through. The greedy pass that picks cone roots runs in criticality order, not blocksWorkDepth order — otherwise a high-criticality epic could be swallowed into another epic's cone and never appear as a row. The next-hop pick uses criticality too.
  • Placement: a “Critical paths” button and dropdown in the Auto-Scheduler controls, with a button through to the full report. The collapsed <details> at the bottom of AutoScheduler.tsx goes away; focusing moves into the dropdown, the full listing becomes the standalone report.
  • Keep the work/queued split, labeled honestly. It answers "shorten this chain by cutting dependencies, or by adding capacity?" — the most actionable thing the trace produces — so it stays, scoped explicitly to in-plan queueing.

6 · Still open

  • Does the standalone report include the team-load section? It's derived from the same trace, but it is arguably its own report.
  • Shared simulation across a document. Three embedded critical-path reports over the same JQL currently means three Monte Carlos. There's precedent for collapsing this — ChildQueryGroups already dedupes identical Jira requests.
  • Slack as a later addition. How far a chain can slip before the finish date moves is the classic complement to criticality, is a property of the schedule rather than a claim about teams, and falls out of the same trace. Deliberately not in v1.
  • Chains that leave the query. linkDirectBlocks drops blockers whose keys aren't in the result set, so some chains dead-end for a reason we should say out loud rather than render as "starts here."
  • Pre-existing bug, out of scope. resetLinkedIssue calls getEstimationData(issue, {}), which skips the user's configured story-point and confidence defaults and pins uncertaintyWeight to the function's hardcoded 80.
  • Texture stays opt-in. The validated hue pair has wide CVD margins, so no hatching by default; a 45° texture is available for print and forced-colors.
