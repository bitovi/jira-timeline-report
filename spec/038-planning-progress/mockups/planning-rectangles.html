Planning Progress — visual explorations

Mockups for turning the Estimation Progress report into a planning progress report. Every idea below renders the same example portfolio so they can be compared directly. Nothing here is decided — these are options to react to.

0 · The rung ladder

One ladder, applied at every level of the hierarchy. Every planning rung is still statusCategory: To Do — so the planning ramp lives entirely inside Atlaskit's neutral band, deepening as readiness increases. Blue and green keep their Jira meanings and sit past the divider. Refined is status-only — no Jira field proves a solution is specified enough to size.

Left of the divider is planning — all of it still To Do in Jira's eyes, which is exactly why a muted ramp is the honest encoding rather than a stylistic choice. Right of the divider is execution, and the planning questions stop applying.

"Broken down" is not a colour. It was a rung in an earlier draft, but in a fractal the children are drawn underneath the parent — so breakdown is already visible as structure, and colouring it too would say the same thing twice. Dropping it leaves the ramp meaning exactly one thing: the item's own readiness, which is self-evidence and nothing else. Breakdown is the one property that describes a relationship to children, and structure is the right encoding for it.
Rung × depth. "Sized" and "all children sized" are the same rung at different depths. Planning depth is now literally how many rows of rectangles you can see. Where nesting isn't drawn (§F, §G, §H) it appears as a stacked-bar glyph — meaning the plan reaches three levels down.

0b · Work that has already started

Execution is not rung 5. An epic can be In Progress without ever having been sized — and when that happens it is the loudest finding the report can produce. So planning rung and execution state are two independent axes, collapsed to one colour for display, with the disagreement flagged.

The collapse rule

If statusCategory is In Progress or Done, that wins the fill colour — planning readiness is moot once the work is underway. Otherwise the planning rung supplies the fill.

Why it can't just be rungs 5 and 6

Linear ladder — what it would claim

A linear reading implies everything below was satisfied: refined, sized, broken down, scheduled. For this epic none of that is true. The ladder would be quietly lying.

Open question. Should Done work still show an undiscovered-scope gap? C · Timeline placement — the rank-ordered queue

Dated work sits on its real dates. Everything else queues to the right of it in Jira Rank order, sized by whatever estimate exists. Nothing is unplaceable and there is no gutter — the plan simply keeps extending rightward, and how far right it reaches is the forward visibility.

The placement rule, in full.
1 · An item with dates sits on those dates.
2 · The frontier is the latest end date among dated items in that subtree.
3 · Undated siblings queue to the right of the frontier in Rank order, butted end to end.
4 · Width is the estimate converted to team-time via points ÷ pointsPerDayPerTrack. An unestimated item gets a nominal width and is marked as such.
5 · Recurse — children follow the same rule inside their parent's span.

No axis labels on the left — the staircase identifies the levels on its own. Only the top level carries text; everything below is shape, with names on hover. Child counts and states both vary the way they really do: one epic here has 18 stories spread across five different states, its neighbour has 2.

What the three groups tell you. NPS is the healthy shape — four solid levels near term, tapering to three, then two, then nothing as you look right. That taper is correct, not a gap. 1st store loses its dates in December and collapses almost immediately after; note the scheduled epic in Dec with no stories under it at all, which is the kind of thing this view is for. Agentic FNOL has no dates anywhere, so its frontier sits at today and every level below the outcome is hatched — an outcome that exists on paper and nowhere else. The amber line lands in a different place in each group because the frontier is computed per subtree.

Parking undated work in a sidebar makes it a pile with no duration and no order, so you can't tell whether it's two weeks or two years of work. The queue answers that; the gutter can't.

The honest caveat. The queue is only as good as its assumptions — it implies strict sequential delivery with no parallelism, so the projected tail is a worst case rather than a forecast. Re-ranking a backlog silently reshuffles everything right of the frontier. That's defensible if the projected region is permanently and visibly marked as inferred, which is what the hatching is for.

D · The squeeze — overlapping children

Real children overlap in time, so they can't tile cleanly under a parent bar. The resolution: bars sit in representative slots by default, and on hover a bar expands to its true dates and rises above its siblings. The axis is never actually broken — it's just deferred until you ask.

F · The wedge — depth vs. time distance

The guidance that falls out of the fractal: planning depth should decrease with time distance. Near work deep, far work shallow. Each cell carries two things — colour is the dominant readiness of work scheduled into that window, and the glyph is how many levels down the breakdown reaches. Biggest unresolved question. A2/A3 require parents to hold a meaningful rough estimate after they've been broken down. Worth checking against a real instance before any of this is specced.
