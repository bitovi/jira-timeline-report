Forge navigation — killing the double sidebar

Jira's nav4 gives every app a persistent left sidebar. Ours renders a second one inside the iframe, so users get two vertical rails and about 390 px of chrome before the report starts. Forge's jira:globalPage can host sub-pages in Jira's own sidebar — so the fix is to hand our settings pages to Jira and keep only Sources in the report, where it belongs.

Platform reality check. Sub-pages are a Forge-only capability. jira:globalPage accepts pages (flat) or sections (grouped with headers); Connect's generalPages has only location + weight and cannot nest. Connect is also end-of-support. So this design lands with the Forge migration (spec/021-forge) and Connect keeps today's in-app sidebar unchanged.

2 — What Forge gives us

jira:globalPage renders sub-items under the app's entry in Jira's rail. Each route is appended to /jira/apps/{appId}/{envId} and we resolve it internally with view.createHistory(). Two shapes are available.

The parent item is the report. Clicking Status Reports goes to the global page root; a Report sub-page would be a second route to the same destination. Jira's own Spaces nav doesn't do this — "Itsy Marketplace" is the link to the space, the chevron is a separate expand control, and the children are boards. Ours matches: no Report child.

The two headers now name a real behavioural split, not just a topic. Both sections are still plain Forge routes — the difference is what we render for them. 

modules:
  jira:globalPage:
    - key: status-reports
      resource: main
      render: native
      layout: native
      title: Status Reports          # route "" — the report itself
      sections:
        - header: Report settings    # we render these OVER the report
          pages:
            - { title: Sources, route: sources, icon: .../search.svg }
            - { title: Timing,  route: timing,  icon: .../calendar.svg }
        - header: Global settings    # we render these INSTEAD OF the report
          pages:
            - { title: Teams,    route: teams,    icon: .../team.svg }
            - { title: Features, route: features, icon: .../spark.svg }
            - { title: Theme,    route: theme,    icon: .../brush.svg }
            - { title: Storage,  route: storage,  icon: .../db.svg }
        - header: Help
          pages:
            - { title: Guides & support, route: help, icon: .../help.svg }
The rule: report settings open over the report, global settings replace it. Forge only gives us routes — it has no opinion about what a route renders. So /sources and /timing render the report with a rail open, while /teams and friends render a page. Sources is the panel you touch every session and its whole job is to change what you're looking at; losing the report to edit it is the thing we're fixing. Timing has the same character, so it gets the same treatment.
This makes the second rail transient rather than permanent — which was the actual problem. Sections 3 and 4 are the placements for that rail, plus the alternative of not having one at all.

3 — Option A: Sources opens over the report

The report stays mounted and visible; Sources is a transient rail you open, edit, Apply, and dismiss. Three placements, differing mainly in what opens it. A3 is the recommendation — read A1 and A2 first for the contrast.

vs. A1 Why A3 wins What it costs
Discovery Sources sits with Timing/Teams/Theme where people already look for configuration, instead of being a lone button hiding among report controls. —
Consistency All six settings are nav items. The Report-settings group shares a behaviour (opens a rail), not just a header. Two nav items in the same list behave differently — Sources keeps your report, Teams replaces it. The section headers have to carry that.
Controls bar Stays clean. No seventh control competing with Filters and View settings. —
The seam risk — Jira's rail and our rail are in different documents. Background, top alignment, borders and shadows have to line up or it reads as two competing rails again — the exact thing we're removing. We only control our half. A1's right drawer is bounded by our own iframe edge and has no seam.
Spike dependency risk — A1's trigger is in-app: no route change, works regardless of what Forge does on navigation. A3 makes the control you touch every session a route change. If sidebar navigation remounts the app (§7), A3 is dead and A1 is not.
Both are transient. Neither A1 nor A3 shows a second rail at rest — that was the real problem, and both solve it. The choice between them is placement and risk, not permanence.

Recommendation: 2b + A3, with A1 as the fallback. Grouped Forge sections where Report settings open a rail over the report and Global settings replace it. Sources and Timing live in the nav with everything else, the controls bar stays clean, and the second rail appears only while you're configuring.

A1 is the same panel on the other side, triggered in-app. It is strictly more robust — no seam, no route change — so if the §7 spike shows navigation remounts the app, fall back to A1 and leave Sources out of the nav. B2's collapsed chip row remains a good later addition to either: a one-line summary of the active query above the controls bar, whose "Edit" opens the same rail.

7 — Verify before building

The whole design rests on one unverified assumption: clicking a sub-page must not remount the app. Our report costs a full issue load — tens of seconds on a large JQL. If sidebar navigation reloads the iframe, A3 is dead (its trigger is a route change), and every settings panel becomes an in-app drawer over a single report page — A1, extended to all six. Prove this with a throwaway Forge app on a dev site before any of this is planned.
Question What the docs imply How to prove it
Does a sidebar click remount the iframe? No. view.createHistory() returns a listen(location, action) callback, which is pointless across a reload; the global page doc says the sidebar "will only change the global page URL, you will need to handle routes inside your app"; the official tutorial feeds listen into React Router's <Router> and only unsubscribes on window.unload, described as the user leaving the app entirely. Module-scope let mounts = 0; mounts++ plus a console.log in history.listen. Click between two sub-pages: the counter must stay at 1 and the listener must fire.
Does the query string survive? highest risk Unknown. LocationDescriptor is { pathname, search, hash, state }, but a sidebar click almost certainly pushes only a pathname. Our entire report state lives in query params — if search is dropped, a round trip to Teams silently wipes the user's report. That is worse than a reload: it's data loss, not latency. Load a sub-page with ?foo=bar, click to another sub-page and back, and log history.location.search at each step.
Do we remount ourselves? Our problem, not Forge's. If each route mounts its own React tree, the QueryClient must live above the router or returning to Report refetches everything regardless of what the iframe does. Architecture review; not a platform question.
Is the parent item clickable? Unknown. Jira's Spaces nav gives the parent a link and a separate chevron. If Forge's parent only expands, we're forced to add a Report sub-page back and live with the redundancy. Deploy sections and click the parent row and its chevron.
Can the rail meet Jira's cleanly? Unknown, and A3-specific. Our iframe's top edge, background and border have to align with Jira's rail or the two read as competing rails. We only control our half. Render a plain left-flush panel in the dev app and look at the seam, in both light and dark theme, at a couple of window widths.

8 — Open questions

  • Recent reports in the nav? Not possible. pages and sections are declared in manifest.yml and are static at deploy time; displayConditions only toggles visibility, it can't render a per-user list. The module that can do this, global:ui, is EAP, UI Kit-only, contractually barred from production, and moves the app out of Jira's nav into the site app switcher entirely. So recents belong in the existing Saved reports menu — and the parent nav item should resume your last-viewed report rather than landing on an empty "Configure a JQL" state.
  • Report-scoped vs. global settings. Sources and Timing are per-report; Teams/Features/Theme/ Storage are per-instance. The rail-vs-page split communicates this, but does a settings page also need a "for: <saved report>" indicator?
  • Does Connect diverge or freeze? Cheapest path is to leave Connect on today's in-app sidebar and build the new shell Forge-only, accepting two layouts until Connect sunsets.
